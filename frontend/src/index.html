A QUICK FIELD GUIDE

Three ways to inspect a claim.

  1. Run a clean clip. Green means the detector did not flag it. A clean recording can still produce a false alert.
  2. Add a supported CAN attack. The attacker emits ordinary encoded frames into the same decoding path. A supported request can lack eligible material.
  3. Inspect a verdict, then the matrix. A scene alert is different from finding the exact forged object. The offline evaluation is separate from your selected clip.

Capability & eligibility

A0 uses unrelated values and timing. A1 understands arrival timing and free slots. A2 also repairs protocol counts and counters. A3 adds plausible physics and continuity. A4 draws on recorded motion.

T3 is inherently A3/A4. T3 and T4 need moving tracks. Support is not a promise that a clip has free slots or eligible trajectories. An emitted attack can escape detection.

Four ways to alter the story.

            

              Explore the attack mechanism. These symbolic paths teach a

            Why an alert? Timing or continuity that differs from
            calibrated clean behavior supports an anomaly finding. Fusion can
            retain an alert after the current reason stops triggering.
          

          

            Why can a ghost be green? Genuine radar artifacts are still
            real sensor output. Static clutter and repeated motion are not
            automatically attacks. Green reports the detector’s decision only.
          

          

            Why can a forgery escape? A plausible trajectory can satisfy
            these checks. Scene detection and exact-object localization differ;
            this prototype cannot identify a human attacker or guarantee
            authenticity.
